## Why your attic needs to breathe

Your attic should stay close to the temperature outside: cool and dry in winter, not baking in summer. When it can't vent, the roof pays the price.

### Hot summers

A closed-up attic gets far hotter than the air outside. That heat cooks your shingles from below, shortens their life and makes the bedrooms upstairs harder and more expensive to cool.

### Damp winters

Warm, moist air from showers, cooking and breathing rises into the attic. Without ventilation it condenses on the cold underside of the roof deck, which leads to mold, rotted plywood and rusty nails. See [roof sheathing explained](/roof-sheathing-explained/).

### Ice dams

A warm attic melts snow on the roof, and the water refreezes at the cold eaves. The ice builds up, water backs up under the shingles and leaks into the walls and ceilings.

### Your roof warranty

Shingle manufacturers, including GAF, require proper attic ventilation for full warranty coverage. As a GAF Master Elite® contractor, we make sure every roof we install is vented the way the warranty expects.

## Signs your attic isn't venting properly

- **Upstairs rooms stay hot** in summer, and the air conditioning runs nonstop
- **Ice dams or long icicles** along the gutters in winter
- **Frost, damp spots or mold** on the underside of the roof boards
- **A musty smell** in the attic or rusty nail tips poking through the deck
- **Shingles curling or blistering** years before they should
- **Peeling paint** on the eaves and soffits

## Attic ventilation services

- **Attic vent installation.** Continuous ridge vents, soffit vents and roof vents, sized for your attic so air comes in at the eaves and goes out at the peak.
- **Attic vent repairs.** Cracked vent covers, worn rubber boots and lifted flashing around vents replaced and sealed, which stops one of the most common roof leaks.
- **Blocked soffit vents.** Insulation pushed against the eaves can block the air coming in. We open the airflow so the whole system works.
- **Ventilation upgrades with a new roof.** The best time to fix ventilation is during a [roof replacement](/residential-roofing/roof-replacement/), when we can cut a continuous ridge vent before the new shingles go on.
- **Free inspection.** We check the roof and the attic and photograph what we find. Our infrared camera also shows hidden damp spots. See [roof inspections](/residential-roofing/roof-inspections/).

## How a balanced system works

Good attic ventilation needs both halves. **Intake vents** low on the roof, usually soffit vents under the eaves, let fresh air in. **Exhaust vents** high on the roof, ideally a ridge vent along the peak, let hot, damp air out. The common building-code rule of thumb is about 1 square foot of net free vent area for every 300 square feet of attic floor, split roughly evenly between intake and exhaust. We also never mix different kinds of exhaust vents on one roof, because a ridge vent and a roof fan, for example, can pull air from each other instead of from the attic.
