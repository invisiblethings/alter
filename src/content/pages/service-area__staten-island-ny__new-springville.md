## Roofing in New Springville: what we see on these homes

New Springville sits in the middle of Staten Island, around the Staten Island Mall and Richmond Avenue. Much of its housing went up in the decades after the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge opened in 1964, which means many original roofs have already been replaced once and the replacements are now reaching the end of their life.

### Townhouses and attached rows

Many New Springville developments are rows of attached townhouses. The roofs share edges with the neighbors, so the flashing where your roof meets the next unit is where most leaks start. We replace one unit at a time and seal it properly into the roofs on either side.

### High-ranch homes

High-ranches often have a low-slope or flat section over the garage next to a pitched shingle roof. The joint between the two is the most common leak we fix in the area, and we rebuild it with new flashing and membrane.

### Detached and semi-detached houses

Colonials, capes and split-levels with asphalt shingle roofs. After 20 years, curling shingles, granules in the gutters and worn pipe boots are the usual signs it's time for a new roof.
