## Why gutters and siding matter for your roof

When gutters clog or pull away, water spills over the edge, soaks the fascia and can back up under the first row of shingles. That causes a leak that looks like a roof problem. Overflowing gutters also pour water down the walls and into the foundation. Siding is the layer that keeps that water out of your walls.

On Staten Island, the pressure comes from all sides. Heavy summer storms overload undersized gutters. Leaves from tree-lined streets in Westerleigh, Todt Hill and Huguenot clog them every fall. Nor'easters drive rain sideways into worn siding, and salt air near the water in South Beach and Great Kills is hard on trim and fasteners.

## Signs it's time to call

- Gutters **overflow** in heavy rain, even when clean
- Gutters **sag** or pull away from the fascia
- **Peeling paint, rot or stains** on fascia, trim or siding
- **Cracked, loose or warped** siding panels after a storm
- **Puddles** along the foundation or a **damp basement** after rain

## Doing it all at once saves money

If you're planning a [new roof](/residential-roofing/roof-replacement/), it's the cheapest time to replace tired gutters and siding. The roof edge is already open, new drip edge and flashing go in properly, and you pay for one setup instead of two. Because we're a roofing company first, we pay attention to where the roof, gutters and walls meet, which is where most water problems start.
