## Spring: recover from winter

Winter is the hardest season on roofs. Once it warms up:

- Look from the ground for **missing, cracked or lifted shingles**
- Check **flashing** around chimneys, vents and skylights
- **Clear gutters and downspouts** of winter debris
- Look in the **attic** for damp spots, stains or daylight
- On flat roofs, look for **ponding, blisters and open seams**
- Book an **inspection** if anything looks off. Spring is the ideal time.

## Summer: heat and storms

- Make sure **attic ventilation** is working; trapped heat ages shingles faster
- Watch for **curling or blistering** shingles on sun-exposed slopes
- Check that **flat roof drains** are clear before summer thunderstorms
- After storms, look for **debris and branch damage**
- Schedule **repairs and replacements** while the weather is reliable

## Fall: prepare for winter

- **Clean gutters** after leaves drop, the most important fall job
- **Trim branches** that hang over or touch the roof
- **Seal small gaps** around flashing and vents before freeze-thaw season
- Check **attic insulation**, which helps prevent ice dams
- Get a **pre-winter inspection**, especially for flat roofs and older roofs

## Winter: watch and react

- Watch for **ice dams** along the roof edge
- After heavy snow, look for **sagging** and stains on top-floor ceilings
- Don't climb on an icy roof; **use a roof rake from the ground** if safe
- After nor'easters, check for **wind damage** and call a roofer for anything you find

## Staten Island-specific risks

**Coastal wind.** Homes along the South and East Shore take the brunt of nor'easters, so wind-rated shingles and well-fastened edges matter.

**Trees.** Older neighborhoods have mature trees that fill gutters and drop branches.

**Freeze-thaw cycles.** Water that gets into small cracks expands when it freezes, widening them every winter.
