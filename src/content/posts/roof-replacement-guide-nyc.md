## What a NYC roof replacement estimate should include

Never accept a verbal-only quote: NYC consumer protection rules require a written contract for home improvement work. Every estimate should itemize:

- **Materials and labor.** The exact shingle or membrane system, underlayment, insulation and flashing.
- **Tear-off and disposal.** How many layers come off, and where the debris goes (dumpster, container or truck).
- **Deck repair.** A per-sheet price for rotted or damaged decking, so there are no surprises once the old roof is off.
- **Access.** Scaffolding, lifts, hoisting or sidewalk protection if the building needs it.
- **Permits and approvals.** DOB permit fees when required, plus board or landmarks paperwork.
- **Warranty, in writing.** Manufacturer coverage on materials and the contractor's own workmanship warranty.

## NYC rules that affect your new roof

### Local Laws 92 and 94 (2019)

These laws require a sustainable roofing zone, meaning solar panels, a green roof or a combination, on new buildings and on roof replacements where the entire existing roof deck is replaced. Your contractor should determine whether they apply before work begins.

### Cool roofs

NYC's energy code requires most new and replacement low-slope roofs to have a reflective, light-colored surface. White TPO meets it naturally; dark membranes usually need a reflective coating. Read more in our [TPO roofing guide](/tpo-roofing-guide/).

### Co-ops and condos

Even when DOB doesn't require a permit, co-op and condo boards require approval. Expect to submit a scope of work, proof of insurance and a contractor application, and allow **2–8 weeks**. We prepare this paperwork for you. See our page for [property managers and boards](/property-managers/).

## How a roof replacement works, step by step

1. **Free inspection and written estimate.** We document the whole roof with photos and send an itemized price, usually within 24 hours.
2. **Material selection.** Choose the system that fits your building, borough, budget and plans (for example, solar later). Compare options in our guide to the [best roofing materials for New York homes](/top-roofing-materials-for-homes-in-new-york/).
3. **Permits and approvals.** DOB filing when required; board or landmarks approvals when applicable.
4. **Site protection.** Tarps, debris control and protection for neighbors and sidewalks, which matters on attached NYC buildings.
5. **Tear-off and deck inspection.** Old roofing comes off; rotted or damaged decking is replaced. See [roof sheathing explained](/roof-sheathing-explained/).
6. **Installation.** Underlayment, insulation, shingles or membrane, flashing and edge details, installed to manufacturer specifications and NYC code. Most roofs are done in one day.
7. **Cleanup and final inspection.** Magnetic nail sweep, debris removal, and DOB sign-off if a permit was filed.
8. **Walkthrough and warranty.** Written warranties on both materials and workmanship.

## How to choose a roofing contractor in NYC

- **An active DCWP license.** A Home Improvement Contractor license is required for residential work in NYC; verify it on the DCWP website. Ours is #2129904-DCWP.
- **Real insurance certificates.** Workers' compensation and liability, on paper, not a verbal yes.
- **Permit authority.** Only licensed contractors can file DOB permits. If a roofer suggests you file it yourself, walk away.
- **A written, itemized contract.** NYC requires written contracts for home improvement work over $200.
- **Manufacturer certification.** Certified installers can offer stronger manufacturer warranties. As a GAF Master Elite® contractor, we offer GAF system warranties of up to 50 years.
- **Local references.** Ask for jobs on buildings like yours, in your borough. Bids far below the others usually mean uninsured labor, cheaper materials or surprise charges later.
