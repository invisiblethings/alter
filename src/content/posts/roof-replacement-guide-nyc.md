## Roof replacement cost by roof size

Size sets the base price of a new roof. These ranges are installed prices from our own NYC jobs at $3–$10 per sq ft for asphalt shingles and $5–$14 per sq ft for a flat EPDM or TPO roof, including tear-off of one layer, new underlayment, flashing and cleanup:

| Roof size | Shingle roof | Flat roof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1,000 sq ft | $3,000–$10,000 | $5,000–$14,000 |
| 1,500 sq ft | $4,500–$15,000 | $7,500–$21,000 |
| 2,000 sq ft | $6,000–$20,000 | $10,000–$28,000 |
| 3,000 sq ft | $9,000–$30,000 | $15,000–$42,000 |

Roof size isn't your home's floor area: a pitched roof is larger than the footprint because of its slope and overhangs. Roofers price by the **square**, which is 100 sq ft of roof, so a shingle roof at $3–$10 per sq ft is about **$300–$1,000 per square**. Jobs in Manhattan and Brooklyn usually land toward the top of each range because of access and attached walls.

## Flat roof replacement cost in NYC

Most row houses, brownstones, attached two-family homes and extensions in Brooklyn, Queens, the Bronx and Manhattan have flat roofs. A new flat roof costs about **$5–$14 per sq ft** installed: EPDM is the lowest-cost option, while TPO and modified bitumen cost more but suit roofs that need a reflective surface or take foot traffic. A typical 1,000 sq ft flat roof costs about $5,000–$13,000 on Staten Island and $8,000–$22,000+ in Manhattan.

On a flat roof, the details drive the price as much as the membrane: parapet walls and their flashing, drains and scuppers, tapered insulation to stop ponding water, and roof hatches or skylights. Wet insulation found during tear-off is replaced, because a new membrane over wet insulation fails early. Read more in our guide to [flat roof repair and replacement in NYC](/flat-roof-repair-and-replacement-in-nyc/) or see our [flat roofing services](/flat-roofing/).

## Commercial and multi-family roof replacement cost

Commercial, mixed-use and multi-family buildings use the same flat roof systems, so the base price per square foot is similar: TPO $9–$14, modified bitumen $9–$13 and PVC $10–$16. A 5,000 sq ft TPO roof, for example, starts around $45,000–$70,000 for materials and labor. On top of that come the things that make commercial jobs different:

- **Insulation upgrades** to meet the NYC Energy Conservation Code.
- **DOB permits**, required for virtually all commercial roof replacements.
- **Access**: cranes or hoists, sidewalk sheds and work-hour limits.
- **Rooftop equipment**: HVAC curbs, vents and solar mounts that need new flashing.
- **Local Laws 92 and 94**, when the job replaces the entire roof deck.

## What changes the price of a new roof

- **Size and shape.** Price scales with square footage and with complexity: valleys, dormers, chimneys and skylights all add labor.
- **Layers to remove.** Many NYC roofs have two or three old layers. Each one adds tear-off and disposal cost, and building codes don't allow a new layer over two or more existing ones.
- **Deck condition.** Rotted sheathing is replaced before the new roof goes on. You only see the full extent once the old roof is off, so ask for a per-sheet price in writing. See [roof sheathing explained](/roof-sheathing-explained/).
- **Access.** Driveway or not, attached or detached, building height, and whether materials need hoisting.
- **Pitch.** Steeper roofs need more safety equipment and take longer.
- **Permits and approvals.** DOB permits ($300–$1,000+ when required), cool-roof requirements, Local Laws 92/94 and co-op or landmark approvals.

## 7 signs you need a new roof

1. **Age.** Asphalt shingle and most flat roofs last about 20–30 years. Past 20, repairs start costing more than they're worth.
2. **Leaks in several places**, or the same leak coming back after repairs.
3. **Curling, cracked or missing shingles** across the roof, and granules collecting in the gutters.
4. **Ponding water** on a flat roof that's still there 48 hours after rain, or blisters and cracks across the membrane.
5. **A soft or sagging deck** when you walk on the roof, or sagging lines from the street.
6. **Daylight or water stains in the attic**, wet insulation or mold on the underside of the deck.
7. **Repair bills adding up.** When you're paying for a repair every season, the money is better spent on a new roof.

Only one or two of these signs, on a roof under about 15 years old, usually means a [roof repair](/roof-repair/) will do. Most leaks cost $300–$1,200 to fix: see the [cost to repair a roof leak](/cost-to-repair-roof-leak/).

## What a NYC roof replacement estimate should include

Never accept a verbal-only quote: NYC consumer protection rules require a written contract for home improvement work. Every estimate should itemize:

- **Materials and labor.** The exact shingle or membrane system, underlayment, insulation and flashing.
- **Tear-off and disposal.** How many layers come off, and where the debris goes (dumpster, container or truck).
- **Deck repair.** A per-sheet price for rotted or damaged decking, so there are no surprises once the old roof is off.
- **Access.** Scaffolding, lifts, hoisting or sidewalk protection if the building needs it.
- **Permits and approvals.** DOB permit fees when required, plus board or landmarks paperwork.
- **Warranty, in writing.** Manufacturer coverage on materials and the contractor's own workmanship warranty.

## How to lower the cost of a new roof

- **Inspect early.** Replacing on your schedule is always cheaper than replacing after a ceiling comes down.
- **Repair if you can.** A roof under about 15 years old with one problem area usually just needs a [repair](/roof-repair/).
- **Choose the right material, not the most expensive.** Shingles are excellent value on pitched roofs; EPDM or TPO on flat ones.
- **Bundle the work.** Replace aging [gutters](/siding-gutter/gutter-installation-repair/) and [skylights](/residential-roofing/skylight-installation-repair/) with the roof to avoid paying for access twice.
- **Compare itemized estimates.** A low total that leaves out disposal, deck repair or permits isn't a low price.
- **Ask about offers.** We offer discounts for seniors and veterans; ask when you book your free inspection.

## NYC rules that affect your new roof

### Local Laws 92 and 94 (2019)

These laws require a sustainable roofing zone, meaning solar panels, a green roof or a combination, on new buildings and on roof replacements where the entire existing roof deck is replaced. Your contractor should determine whether they apply before work begins.

### Cool roofs

NYC's energy code requires most new and replacement low-slope roofs to have a reflective, light-colored surface. White TPO meets it naturally; dark membranes usually need a reflective coating. Read more in our [TPO roofing guide](/tpo-roofing-guide/).

### Co-ops and condos

Even when DOB doesn't require a permit, co-op and condo boards require approval. Expect to submit a scope of work, proof of insurance and a contractor application, and allow **2–8 weeks**. We prepare this paperwork for you. See our page for [property managers and boards](/property-managers/).

## How to choose a roofing contractor in NYC

- **An active DCWP license.** A Home Improvement Contractor license is required for residential work in NYC; verify it on the DCWP website. Ours is #2129904-DCWP.
- **Real insurance certificates.** Workers' compensation and liability, on paper, not a verbal yes.
- **Permit authority.** Only licensed contractors can file DOB permits. If a roofer suggests you file it yourself, walk away.
- **A written, itemized contract.** NYC requires written contracts for home improvement work over $200.
- **Manufacturer certification.** Certified installers can offer stronger manufacturer warranties. As a GAF Master Elite® contractor, we offer GAF system warranties of up to 50 years.
- **Local references.** Ask for jobs on buildings like yours, in your borough. Bids far below the others usually mean uninsured labor, cheaper materials or surprise charges later.
