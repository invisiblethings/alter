## How much does a roof replacement cost in NYC?

Most New York City roof replacements land between **$10,000 and $30,000**, with larger, steeper or more complicated roofs going higher. The two things that move the price most are **the material** and **the borough**.

| Material | Per sq ft | 1,200 sq ft roof | Lifespan | Common on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Asphalt shingles | $6–$10 | $7,200–$12,000 | 20–30 yrs | Staten Island, Queens houses |
| EPDM (rubber) | $8–$12 | $9,600–$14,400 | 20–30 yrs | Brooklyn & Queens row homes |
| TPO | $9–$14 | $10,800–$16,800 | 20–30 yrs | Larger flat roofs, commercial |

### What a legitimate estimate includes

Every NYC roof replacement estimate should itemize: materials, labor, tear-off and disposal, debris removal (dumpster or crane truck), scaffolding or lifts if needed, permit fees when required, and flashing and penetration work. NYC consumer protection rules require a written contract for home improvement work, so never accept a verbal-only quote.

## Do you need a permit to replace a roof in NYC?

This is the most-asked question about NYC roofing, and the honest answer is: **it depends on the scope.**

### Usually no permit required

- Like-for-like replacement of the roof **covering**, above the deck
- **No structural changes** to the roof deck or framing
- One- or two-family residential buildings
- The work already meets the NYC Energy Conservation Code

### A permit is required when

- The **structural deck** is replaced or modified
- You're **changing the roof type** (for example, shingles to flat, or one membrane system to another)
- The building is **commercial** (virtually all replacement work)
- The job triggers **Local Laws 92/94** sustainable roofing requirements
- The building is **landmarked** or in a historic district (LPC approval may also be needed)
- **Insulation** is being upgraded to meet current energy code

### How the permit process works

1. **The contractor files.** Your licensed roofer (not you) files through DOB NOW Build. This requires an active DCWP license and DOB registration.
2. **DOB review.** Simple projects can be approved the same day; complex ones, or those needing an architect or engineer, can take two to six weeks.
3. **Inspections.** DOB may inspect at certain stages. Your contractor coordinates them.
4. **Sign-off.** A final inspection closes the permit and leaves a clean record for when you sell.

**Skipping a required permit** risks DOB violations, fines and trouble at sale or refinance. A reputable roofer will tell you up front whether you need one, and include the cost in the estimate.

## NYC rules that affect your new roof

### Local Laws 92 and 94 (2019)

These laws require a sustainable roofing zone, meaning solar panels, a green roof or a combination, on new buildings and on roof replacements where the entire existing roof deck is replaced. Your contractor should determine whether they apply before work begins.

### Cool roofs

NYC's energy code requires most new and replacement low-slope roofs to have a reflective, light-colored surface. White TPO meets it naturally; dark membranes usually need a reflective coating. Read more in our [TPO roofing guide](/tpo-roofing-guide/).

### Co-ops and condos

Even when DOB doesn't require a permit, co-op and condo boards require approval. Expect to submit a scope of work, proof of insurance and a contractor application, and allow **2–8 weeks**. A good contractor prepares this paperwork for you. See our page for [property managers and boards](/property-managers/).

## Signs you need a replacement, not a repair

Before spending $10,000+, make sure you actually need a new roof. Many NYC roofs that look bad can be [repaired](/roof-repair/) for a fraction of the cost. Replacement is usually the right call when:

- The roof is **20+ years old** and has problems in several places
- **Leaks keep coming back** in different spots
- The deck feels **soft or sags**, or you can see daylight in the attic
- Shingles are **curling, cracked or bald** across large areas
- A flat roof has **widespread blisters, splits or wet insulation**
- There are already **two or more layers** of old roofing

## The NYC roof replacement process

1. **Inspection and written estimate.** The full roof is documented with photos and you get an itemized price, ideally within 24 hours.
2. **Material selection.** Choose the system that fits your building, borough, budget and plans (for example, solar later). Compare options in our guide to the [best roofing materials for New York homes](/top-roofing-materials-for-homes-in-new-york/).
3. **Permits and approvals.** DOB filing when required; board or landmarks approvals when applicable.
4. **Site protection.** Tarps, debris control and protection for neighbors and sidewalks, which is essential on attached NYC buildings.
5. **Tear-off and deck inspection.** Old roofing removed; rotted or damaged decking replaced. See [roof sheathing explained](/roof-sheathing-explained/).
6. **Installation.** Underlayment, insulation, membrane or shingles, flashing and edge details installed to manufacturer specifications and NYC code.
7. **Cleanup and final inspection.** Magnetic nail sweep, debris removal, and DOB sign-off if a permit was filed.
8. **Walkthrough and warranty.** Written warranties on both materials and workmanship.

## How to choose a roofing contractor in NYC

Use this checklist before you hire anyone:

- **Active DCWP Home Improvement Contractor license.** Required for residential work in NYC; verify it on the NYC DCWP website. (Ours is #2129904-DCWP.)
- **Workers' compensation and liability insurance.** Ask for the actual certificates, not a verbal yes.
- **Permit authority.** Only licensed contractors can file DOB permits. If a roofer suggests you file it yourself, walk away.
- **Written, itemized contract.** NYC requires written contracts for home improvement work over $200.
- **Manufacturer certification.** Certified installers (such as GAF Master Elite®) can offer stronger manufacturer warranties.
- **Local references** on buildings like yours, in your borough.
- **Be wary of bids far below the others.** They often mean uninsured labor, cheaper materials or "surprise" charges once the old roof is off.
