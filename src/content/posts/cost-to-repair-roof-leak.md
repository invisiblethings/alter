## What makes a roof leak repair cost more or less

Two leaks that look the same from inside can cost very different amounts to fix. These are the things that move the price on our jobs:

- **Where the water gets in.** A pipe boot is a small, quick fix. A valley or a chimney flashing takes more material and more labor.
- **The type of roof.** Shingle roofs and flat roofs use different materials. Flat roofs on attached homes often need the seams and the drainage fixed together.
- **Access.** Steep roofs, three-story homes and Manhattan buildings where materials go up through the building take more setup.
- **Damage underneath.** If water has rotted the plywood deck or soaked the insulation, that part is replaced and priced per sheet, in writing.
- **How many leaks there are.** One leak is a repair. Leaks in several places can mean the roof is near the end of its life.
- **Emergency timing.** Tarping or a temporary seal during a storm is priced separately, before the permanent repair.

## Why waiting makes a roof leak more expensive

A small leak is cheapest to fix the week you notice it. Every storm after that sends more water into the roof deck, the insulation and the drywall underneath. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency advises drying water-damaged materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold. Wet insulation stops insulating, and a deck that stays damp rots, which turns a few-hundred-dollar repair into a deck repair, new drywall and sometimes mold cleanup. If water is coming in right now, [call our emergency line](/roof-repair/emergency-service/) and we'll stop it the same day.

## How we find a roof leak before we fix it

The stain on your ceiling is rarely right under the hole. Water runs along rafters and insulation first, often several feet. That's why so many "repaired" roofs keep leaking: the patch went where the stain was, not where the water got in.

- **Infrared (thermal imaging) camera.** Wet insulation, decking and drywall hold heat differently from dry materials, so hidden moisture shows up on the camera even before a stain appears.
- **Roof and attic check.** We follow the water from the stain back to its source and check the flashing, vents, valleys and seams around it.
- **Photos of the cause.** You see exactly where the water gets in before you approve anything.
- **A written price.** Usually within 24 hours, itemized, with no obligation.

## Can a handyman fix a roof leak?

Sometimes a handyman can stop a drip for a while. But most leaks come from flashing, valleys, vent boots and flat-roof seams, and fixing them properly takes roofing materials and experience: new metal flashing, a new boot woven into the shingles, a welded or adhered seam. Caulk and roofing tar smeared over the spot usually fail within a season and can make the proper repair harder. In New York City, anyone doing home improvement work, including roof repairs, should hold an NYC DCWP Home Improvement Contractor license and carry insurance. Ask for the license number before anyone climbs your roof. Ours is #2129904-DCWP.

## Does homeowners insurance cover roof leaks?

Usually only when the leak was caused by sudden damage: wind tearing off shingles, a fallen tree, hail or a storm. Most policies don't cover leaks from age, worn-out materials or lack of maintenance. If you plan to file a claim, photograph the damage inside and out before anything is moved, and ask your roofer for a written report of the cause. We photograph and document storm damage on every leak call, so you have what your insurer asks for. Your own policy has the final word on what's covered.

## How to compare roof leak repair quotes

Homeowners often get three quotes for the same leak that are hundreds of dollars apart. The difference is usually in what each roofer plans to do, not just the price. Before you choose, check that each quote answers these questions:

- **Did they find the source?** The quote should name where the water gets in, ideally with photos, not just "patch leak."
- **Repair or cover-up?** New flashing or a new boot lasts. Caulk or tar over the old parts usually doesn't.
- **Damaged decking.** Is there a price per sheet if rotted wood is found, so there are no surprises?
- **Warranty in writing.** How long is the repair guaranteed, and what happens if it leaks again?
- **License and insurance.** An NYC DCWP license number and proof of insurance.
- **Cleanup.** Is debris removal included?

A much lower quote often means a caulk patch, no license or no warranty. A much higher one may be a full replacement pitched as the only option. If a repair will do, an honest roofer will say so.
